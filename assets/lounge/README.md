# Video-reference lounge

The editable [lounge.blend](lounge.blend) reconstructs the major furnishings and their relative layout from the supplied `n.mp4` walkthrough. [layout.json](layout.json) records game-space dimensions, landing heights and collision footprints. Dimensions are estimated from the video, not measured.

The main lounge retains oak boards, walnut cubbies with a black grid partition, the cat mural and overhead greenery, the olive sofa, four gray floor chairs, triangular black tables, flower beds, the wooden cat pod, climbing trees, cream loveseat and split entrance curtain. The two central chairs marked for removal in the user's annotation are absent, along with their floor shadows and navigation obstacles. The connected window alcove includes broad drawer cabinets with a green perch mat, a step, two high-backed recliners and footstools. Ceiling tiles, black track lights and warm perimeter strips complete the shell.

People, real cats, toys, loose retail merchandise, bags and cables are omitted. The mural texture is a cropped frame from the supplied walkthrough. Distant buildings are simplified geometry; areas outside the walkthrough and obscured surfaces are approximated. The original video remains in the user's Downloads folder and is not bundled into the game.

The user's [annotated layout](layout-reference.png) establishes a 90° right turn around the vertical axis from the cat-poster view to the window area. The window wing is on the right, with the cream loveseat in front of its opening and the access aisle to its left. The green sofa runs along the near wall. This supersedes the initial interpretation that placed the window alcove behind the viewer.

The [sofa and door annotation](sofa-door-reference.png) reduces the green sofa to two seats and places a closed glass door in a real opening in the near wall beside it. Above the entry cabinet, a simple raised charcoal sign follows the supplied [Namaöste logo](namaoste-logo-reference.png). `scripts/lounge-entry-details.py` authors the entry details; the sofa collision footprint and cat landing route match the smaller seating area. The logo uses solid extruded contours, including the original script, paw dots and Chinese lettering. `namaoste-logo-contours.json` is bundled so the Blender build needs no tracing dependency; regenerate it with `python scripts/trace-lounge-logo.py` (OpenCV) if the reference changes.

Coordinates use Three.js conventions: +Y up, −Z toward the poster, and +X into the window wing. The main lounge spans X −7.8…5 and Z −8…8. The right wing spans X 5…9.5 and Z −2.5…7.5. A large clear glass divider fills X 5.05, Z 0.55…6.1, below height 4.1; the passage remains at its left end, Z −1.1…0.55. Blender's source uses Z up and the exporter converts back to the game's axes. The initial eye position is (0, 1.65, 4.8), where turning 90° right faces the wing.

The [depth and wheel annotation](wheel-depth-reference.png) extends the sofa-to-door span by 2.8 m. Left-wall furnishings, the entry door and reception cabinet move with the wall; the two-seat sofa stays its original size. A white open exercise wheel occupies the new gap. The [sunroom approach annotation](sunroom-wall-reference.png) adds a full-height projecting plaster wall with a return into the sunroom entrance, widened by the shelf correction below. The side recess marked X is sealed off and excluded from floor and camera navigation. `scripts/lounge-depth-wheel.py` authors these additions.

The wheel has a stationary roller cradle and a separately exported `CatWheelRotor`. Cats choose it through the furniture routine, or the player can tap the rim/base in free play's pet mode to invite the selected cat. One cat reserves it through approach, running, braking and exit. Simulation-owned tread speed drives both the running stride and drum rotation; pausing freezes both, and care or wand interruptions return the cat to the floor after braking. Its shared dimensions and route are in `layout.json`; `src/cat-wheel.ts` and `src/cat-behavior.ts` own the running behavior.

The [feeding shelf close-up](feeding-shelf-reference.jpg), from `room-reference/time-52.jpg`, corrects the shelf to the broad front face of the sunroom approach. That face spans X 1.3…5.1 at Z −1.1; rear chairs, bed and cat pod move 2.1 m left to clear the widened wall. The 3.2 m oak-and-white tubular shelf faces the lounge, with a black Marshall Bluetooth speaker on top and nine actual cat reference photographs across its middle. `scripts/lounge-feeding-shelf.py` authors it. Free-play bowls occupy the open floor below it; their shared row in `layout.json` supplies both visible bowls and feeding approaches, with one bowl per resident and no duplicate decorative bowls. The sunroom doorway stays open at its right-hand end.

The rear cat poster, surround, ledge and greenery are centered together on the exposed wall between the cubbies (X −6.4) and projecting wall (X 1.3). `scripts/lounge-center-mural.py` derives the midpoint from the layout and sets the actual artwork center to X −2.55, so repeat builds cannot leave it hidden behind the widened wall.

The two sunroom recliner sofas sit with their backs close to the dividing glass and face +X toward the exterior window, with footstools in front. The cream loveseat and its draped blanket stay on the main-room side. The window access step is beside the seating at Z 1.6. `scripts/lounge-sunroom-layout.py` authors this arrangement, and floor navigation and camera boundaries both respect the glass and its open side passage.

The exterior window uses clear glass, charcoal aluminum mullions, rubber seals and handles. Its former blue backdrop and flat skyline are removed. `scripts/lounge-window-exterior.py` builds two rows of solid city buildings beyond the glass, with facade windows, rooftop structures and visible depth. The city is an approximation, not a reconstruction of a specific real skyline.

`src/room-lighting.ts` reads the device's local wall clock independently of the game timer. Dawn blends from 06:00 to 07:30, dusk from 17:30 to 19:00, and the sky/daylight/city window glow update during play, pause and after resume. These are fixed local-hour transitions without location or weather access. Actual warm ceiling/track lights remain on at all hours; emissive cove strips and a small indoor reflection map support the glass and materials. Blender lights are excluded from the game export, so the runtime lights are authored explicitly.

Rebuild from the repository root:

```powershell
& 'C:\Program Files\Blender Foundation\Blender 5.2\blender.exe' --background --python scripts/build-lounge.py
```

The script creates a new scene, saves the editable source, exports `public/models/lounge.glb`, records geometry statistics in `model-stats.json`, and renders `artifacts/lounge/lounge-main.png` and `lounge-window.png`. Existing scenes are not edited. The source retains named furniture parts and bevel modifiers. Runtime copies are evaluated and merged by material; cameras and lights are excluded from the GLB. Production builds gzip the GLB losslessly using the existing model pipeline.

Append `-- --export-only` to skip reference renders during iteration. Static meshes and rotating wheel meshes are batched separately so the drum keeps its pivot in the game export.

The game loads the lounge independently of the cat models. A neutral shell keeps care playable during loading. Furniture routes match actual perches and open cubbies, and floor navigation uses the obstacle footprints to avoid seating and tables. The `rug` behavior ID now means grooming on the bare oak floor, preserving its internal identifier without rendering a rug.

The [pod annotation](pod-reference.png) defines a hollow bentwood shell with an actual round side passage and a separate transparent glass top. It has an interior cushion and four wooden legs. `scripts/lounge-hollow-pod.py` authors its inner and outer walls; the top has no opaque cap. The blue annotation indicated clear glass, not a blue-colored surface. The [seating annotation](seating-reference.png) identifies the two removed floor chairs.

The [window-tree and blanket close-up](window-tree-blanket-reference.png) informs two separate authored details in `scripts/lounge-furnishing-details.py`: the window tree has forked natural branches, foliage, a pitched wooden house with real round openings, a short ladder, an oval hammock, a clear bowl and a low nest. It does not reuse the entrance tree. The large patterned blanket is a continuous mesh draped over the loveseat's crest, back, seat and front edge, with folds, a rolled hem and hanging fringe. Its flat woven texture is generated by `scripts/make-lounge-blanket-texture.py`; the pattern is not raised geometry.
